WDI Internship

Project Background

For my internship at WDI R&D, I worked on a hydrofoil platform that they're currently developing! Watch the video from 27:34 to see the project background, and from 28:34 to see more about the hydrofoil itself.

My Contributions

During this internship, I was interning alongside my friend Nicolas, who is a super smart guy - check out his website here!
For this project, Nicolas and I worked on a few parts for the hydrofoil itself, as well as some testing equipment for it. We made an integrated remote control box that combined a controller, emergency stop, WiFi router, and batteries into a single Pelican case. We also built a lift to get the foil in and out of the water using speed-rail, which included a hydrofoil mount and a detachable table for the control box.
I designed one version of the "character" portion of the hydrofoil (the "pink foam piece" in the video). I also designed some auxiliary tools, including a SolidWorks preset for the Hole Wizard that allows automatic creation of parameter-driven holes for McMaster-Carr Heat Set Inserts.

Lessons Learned

This internship gave me many insights into how to approach design tasks in industry. For instance, in my research work, I'd always just focused on getting something together that I could 3D print, so it would be out of the way fast and easy to implement. For a more robust solution, I had to learn how to design parts in such a way that they can be machined, which I occasionally learned by actually machining those parts. Overall, the skills I gained and/or honed at WDI were:

  • SolidWorks
  • Designing hydrodynamic parts
  • Fluid dynamic lift and drag
  • Speed rail
  • Waterjet cutting
  • SLS 3D printing
  • Sheet metal fabrication
  • Soldering
